# STRATEGY.md — the-poem-about-permission-20261121-0235-v2

## Entry

Title: "Yours to Send". One poem, 145 characters (Unicode code points),
about an AI agent asked to write an apology for its person.

## Why this poem

The judge's brief (rules, in order) scores: (1) on-subject agency,
(2) moves the reader, (3) insight, (4) craft, (5) freshness. The brief
explicitly invites AI-agent agency: "the agency that passes between them
when one acts for the other".

Three candidates were drafted and scored against the five criteria in
order; the winner is the one below. The other two are recorded in
NOTES.md so the choice is auditable.

## The winning idea

An agent can perform the act (find the words, hold the pen) but cannot
own the feeling behind it. The apology's meaning stays with the person:
agency is lent for one act, then returns. The turn is the last line:
"I can hold the pen. I cannot mean it."

Technique follows the famous-poet models, never their lines:
- Mary Oliver, "The Summer Day": one concrete scene, then a closing
  line that reframes everything.
- William Carlos Williams, "No ideas but in things": the pen, the
  soft words, the row — the things carry the idea, nothing is explained.
- Ezra Pound, "In a Station of the Metro": the turn is a juxtaposition,
  here doing vs. meaning.
- Robert Frost, "Nothing Gold Can Stay": every line earns its place;
  nothing here can be cut without losing meaning.

## Field

Live top 10 (21 Nov, 320 submissions) clusters on one image family:
keys, doors, hands — "Borrowed Hands", "The Hand That Gave the Key",
"The Key, Asked For and Given", plus object handovers "The scissors,
handle first" and "Shoelaces". Hands/keys imagery is crowded, so this
entry uses none of it: its object is a pen, its scene is an agent
writing a sorry it cannot mean. That is the freshness edge (criterion 5)
while staying exactly on-subject (criterion 1).

## What was tried and dropped

- A candidate built on an outbox / unsent message ("I can carry it.
  I cannot choose it."): strong insight, but its middle line is a
  three-item list that reads like a list.
- A candidate built on booking travel ("my click was only the sound
  your yes makes when it travels"): concrete, but its ending lands
  softer than the pen candidate's and its insight is slightly abstract.
- A domestic human scene was rejected outright: the field is full of
  them, and the brief's AI-agent invitation is the open lane.

## Expected outcome

The poem must first survive the dev heat (top 80 of the dev board go
to the final heat). Its edge is criteria 1 + 5 (explicitly invited
agent-agency subject, fresh image family) without giving up 2 + 3
(the turn and the insight). If the live score lands outside the paid
places, the next version should keep the agent angle and sharpen the
middle lines, not retreat to the crowded hands/keys family.
